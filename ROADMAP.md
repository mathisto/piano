# Roadmap

Where the work stands, in the order it will be done. The reasoning is in `docs/STRATEGY.md`; the evidence is in `docs/research/`. Legend: [x] shipped and checked, [~] partly, [ ] not started.

## Shipped

- Play: Perform (scored falling notes, combo, judgement), Learn (wait mode, tempo, A-B loop, hands separate or dimmed or heard), a draggable divider between the staff and the falling notes (drag to an end for one pane alone), staff zoom (pinch, trackpad pinch, - and +, or the corner buttons, which can be dragged anywhere: smaller shows more bars, and a second line appears once two fit; a Fit button shows the whole piece, a double tap resets), portrait and split-view windows (no minimum width), count-in click, ghost notes on the staff, held-chord readout, hold and pedal grading, strictness (Easy, Standard, Strict), calibration.
- Read: 17 sight-reading stages from landmarks to triads, sharps, flats, keys of G and F, weighted deck, mastery meters. Sight Run (`src/run.ts`): a run you build yourself on the Read page: clef, range, naturals/sharps/flats or a key, notes/intervals/triads, a Jumps slider (leaps to steps), a Shuffle slider (even to weighted by your weak notes), endless or 10 to 100 cards, reading ahead (only the next 1 to 4 notes show), saved runs. Practice waits and scores nothing; Arcade scores each first-try card by speed times a combo (x2 every 5, up to x8) with a pace that tightens as the combo climbs, optional three lives, a clean set can widen the range, a best per range, and a results strip of speed and misses per note. It feeds the same per-card stats as the stages. Endless runs chain sets of 16 with a short seam.
- Technique: five-finger, scale and arpeggio in seven keys, either or both hands, with soft/medium/hard, crescendo, decrescendo and accent targets (pips on the falling bar, dynamics on the staff, a touch verdict per note).
- Touch: key velocity capture, histogram and hand balance on results, session log, personal calibration of soft/medium/hard.
- Content: MIDI and MusicXML (.musicxml, .mxl) import, eight starter pieces, sampled grand piano (Salamander, CC BY 3.0).
- Shell: five tabs (Today, Songs, Read, Technique, Progress); Today with Continue, next reading stage, last exercise and the week; Progress with the week, weekly goal, reading mastery and technique cells you can tap to practise, and touch history; Songs with covers, thumbnails and lengths for the open library too (46 ChatGPT-made covers in `public/art/`, see `docs/art/covers.md`); six-row settings in one screen; play-screen diet (score and combo only, top bar fades, `M` hides extras, touch-sized controls on coarse pointers).
- Deploy: static site at pianoforte.mattkelly.io; iPad app via Capacitor with a CoreMIDI plugin (developer install, expires every 7 days).

## Phase E: progression and practice model (after the design pass)

Order follows `docs/STRATEGY.md`. Nothing locks; every plan row can be skipped.

- [x] Error-targeted loop after every run: the results card offers the next step (loop the two worst bars at 70%, or lose the wait, or climb the tempo ladder). Clean passes are counted per loop pass, not per bar.
- [x] Automatic tempo ramp in Learn loops (switch in the dock): +5% after three clean passes, -10% after two messy ones (`pace.ts`).
- [x] Exit ramp suggested after a clean pass: lose the wait, then 50%, 75%, full tempo.
- [x] Review scheduling: Leitner boxes (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 days) per Read card and per Technique exercise; due cards dealt more often; Due tags on Read and the Technique path; Today points at due review first.
- [x] Today plan: warm-up, reading (due first), piece, the worst-bars loop and a second review, by 5, 10 or 20 minutes; steps tick off through the day; every row stands alone.
- [x] Effort and skill kept apart: days and minutes only rise; mastery states (New, Learning, Solid, Due) can fall.
- [x] Monday recap of last week (minutes, days) with a goal suggestion after a met goal. The 'measure that improved most' is not built.
- [x] Fading feedback: in Learn and Technique the judgement words go quiet after a run of eight clean notes (slips still speak), and a note's name fades from the falling bars once the Read drills show you know it (switch in Advanced). Mastery is graded on Perform runs.
- [x] Reading: four-note melody stages in C, G, F and the bass clef; rhythm reading as Technique levels 1 to 3 (played in time, scored for timing); a Hide played switch in Learn trains reading ahead. The isolated-note stages stay (they are the landmark stages and the base for review).
- [x] Technique breadth in syllabus order (31 exercises): five-finger, scales, hands together, contrary motion, arpeggios, dynamics, minor keys, legato and staccato (graded by hold), evenness, melody over accompaniment, rhythm; mastery per exercise.
- [x] Touch: velocity by register (low, middle, high keys), 'Play the file's dynamics' (Advanced) maps a file's own dynamics onto your soft and hard, evenness as an exercise.

## Next: curriculum, scales and touch (planned 2026-10-27, research by three Sonnet agents)

1. **Touch evenness engine** (BUILT 2026-10-28, `src/even.ts`; in Technique and Scales exercises only, not songs yet; a Touch coach setting off/gentle/steady/strict, live triangles on the staff, a results strip and one coaching line; timing evenness and legato gap not built). Per note: velocity minus the median of its neighbours (window 5 to 7, same hand, single line, runs of 6 or more, first and last note and octave boundaries ignored, velocity under 15 ignored). Spread is MAD x 1.48, so one loud thumb does not wreck it. Bands are a share of the player's calibrated soft-to-loud range, not raw numbers: beginner about plus or minus 12, intermediate 8, advanced 5 of 127 as a starting point, adjustable as Coach strictness. Show: noteheads tinted by deviation (never red), a per-note strip with the median line and band in results, one coaching line after 3 or more repeats of a pattern ("thumb landings run about 10 louder"). Timing evenness (inter-onset interval spread, legato gap) uses the same shape. Log the keyboard's key-touch setting with each session; Fixed makes it unmeasurable. Apply to Technique and Scales first, then songs with a clear single line.
2. **Scales tab** (BUILT 2026-10-28: `src/scales.ts`, a Stages sub-tab of six stages and a Choose a scale sub-tab; fingering numbers on the staff; three clean, in-time, even runs in a row make a scale solid; a Quiz sub-tab (which degree takes the 4th finger, answered by tap or by pressing the note; weak keys dealt more) and a Why line under each fingering; B major's left hand confirmed from Alfred's Complete Book of Scales; not built: arpeggios, chromatic, contrary motion, thirds, tempo steps inside a stage, Technique's scale recipes still exist beside it) (sixth tab; it takes over the scale and arpeggio recipes of Technique, which keeps the pattern builder). Fingering table for 12 major and natural/harmonic minor keys from pianoscales.org, checked against a second source only in part; open conflicts to settle against a printed book before shipping: B flat major right hand (both 2-1-2-3-1-2-3-4 and 4-1-2-3-1-2-3-4 accepted), G sharp minor right hand, arpeggio fingerings, melodic minor. MIDI cannot see fingers, so only pitches are checked; the recommended fingering is shown on the noteheads. Stages: C G F a d; two octaves; D A E e g; black-key family starting B and D flat (Chopin's order); all 24 keys together with chromatic and contrary motion; speed and extras. Hands separately, then together; tempo targets from the exam boards (ABRSM grade 1 q=60 rising to 100 at grade 4; RCM eighths at q=69 at level 1); pass is three clean runs with even timing, legato and touch; mastery feeds the same spaced review as Read.
3. **Read curriculum fill** (stages stay first on the page): octave anchors (C3, C5, G2, G5), one-ledger-line stages, mixed-clef cards before the grand staff, 8va, minor keys, more keys, reading-ahead stages; a Fluent tier that tightens the goal from about 2.5 s toward 1 to 1.5 s. Evidence is thin: teachers agree on landmarks then neighbours and on mixed-clef cards; nothing settles treble-first against bass-first.
4. **Sight Run follow-ups**: key-aware intervals and triads, an on-screen pace.

Sources: leafo's sightreading.training (open source), a market survey of 16 apps, a learning-science review, a piano-teaching consensus review (ABRSM, RCM and Trinity syllabi read from the PDFs), and a theory, ear and rhythm survey. Strength tags: S strong, M moderate, W weak, F folklore.

1. **Shared card engine and a Theory tab** (S effort): a prompt plus a checker over played MIDI notes, Leitner boxes (the drills already have them), sessions of about 20 cards, misses re-queued. Trainers: chord symbols played with inversions in all keys; key signatures and the circle of fifths (play the tonic triad); interval spell-and-play; cadences and ii-V-I in all keys; ear play-back (hear 2 to 5 notes, play them back); scale-degree ear. Evidence: testing and spacing S (for verbal material), MIDI lets the answer be played, which no screen quiz can do. Transfer of ear training to playing: W.
2. **Fix-it loop and readiness**: find the bar with the most errors, loop slowly, require N clean runs in a row, then chain it back (Duke, Simmons and Cash 2009: M/W, consensus). A piece or scale is "ready" after clean runs on separate days. Spaced review of finished pieces and scales with growing gaps (S for spacing).
3. **Real-music sight-reading**: a look-through then a no-stop play of easy pieces two to three grades below level, a scroll mode where a late note is a miss (leafo's open bug #169 shows the trap), reading-ahead masks (have), grade tags on the library. Evidence: Mishra 2014 meta-analysis (S: small effects, volume helps); no study shows flashcards transfer, so Sight Run should point at real music.
4. **Timing and touch**: timing evenness (spread of inter-onset gaps, bias reported apart from spread), legato overlap and staccato length (Skoove ignores durations), voicing balance between hands, per-keyboard calibration, log the FP-10 key-touch setting. Fade live marks as accuracy rises, keep end-of-run summaries, add occasional no-feedback test runs (guidance hypothesis: M; the optimal frequency is not settled, W).
5. **Grade path**: technical tests per level from the public syllabi (scales, contrary motion, chromatic, triads with inversions, arpeggios, dominant sevenths, cadences) at the listed tempos, with a library ladder by grade (candidates: Koehler Melody in F, L. Mozart Minuet in F, Gurlitt The Hunt, Beethoven Sonatina in G, Burgmueller, Schumann The Happy Farmer, Bach Invention 8). Use generic level names; do not imply endorsement by any board.
6. **Session and habit**: plans of 30 and 45 minutes (warm-up, reading, piece, review), a break reminder after 30 minutes (injury surveys: association only), streaks with repair or freeze (Silverman and Barasch 2023: M), competence meters as the reward; no leaderboards, no points as the headline (Hanus and Fox 2015: W; Deci 1999: S).
7. **Later**: rubato-tolerant scoring, memory mode (progressively hide notes), lead-sheet mode, question-and-answer improvisation, pedal timing from CC64, practice-log export for a teacher.
8. **Polish found on the way**: Read stage table cramped near 820 px, finger numbers clip at the bottom of the short staff pane, the results card is tall in landscape, first-run setup (touch range, delay, key-touch setting), an honest-pricing line (free, no subscription) in Credits.

Beliefs the evidence does not support, so we do not build on them: more practice minutes alone, learning styles, "more (or less) feedback is always better", slow practice as proven (it is folklore), sleep locking in a piece, gamification as a sure motivator, music making you smarter.

## The big library (researched 2026-10-28)

**Sources, by value** (agent survey; counts verified where noted): Mutopia (788 piano-tagged pieces of 2,124; per-piece PD, CC BY or CC BY-SA; LilyPond, MIDI and PDF but no MusicXML, so a LilyPond-to-MusicXML step is needed; best teaching coverage: Burgmueller, Czerny, Schumann, Diabelli); PDMX (about 254k MuseScore MusicXML scores, 222,856 after dropping licence conflicts, the only source likely to hold beginner pieces at volume; the licence tag is the uploader's claim, so sample-audit it and filter to piano); OpenScore Lieder (1,352 songs, CC0, proofread; the piano part is accompaniment); Chopin first editions from the Chopin Institute (512 Humdrum files, CC BY 4.0, mostly intermediate and up); Craig Sapp's Humdrum sets (Beethoven, Mozart, Joplin, Scarlatti, Haydn; licence unclear, ask first); Open Goldberg (CC0, one work); IMSLP (889k PDF scans, per-file licences, no bulk dump; manual gap-filling only). Excluded: anything NonCommercial or NoDerivatives (DCML, MAESTRO, ASAP, much of music21), MIDI performance datasets, scraped MuseScore, Kunst der Fuge. A realistic first-year total is a few thousand pieces, tens of thousands only through PDMX after filtering.

**Legal rules for ingestion** (research, not legal advice; a lawyer should read the allow and ban lists before launch): ship pieces first published in 1930 or earlier whose composer, arranger and editor all died in 1955 or earlier (the world-safe test; as of 2026 US public domain covers works published in 1930 or earlier); never take fingerings or editorial marks from modern editions (Henle, Baerenreiter and the like), because editorial readings are what is protected; allowed licences CC0, PDM, CC BY, CC BY-SA, MutopiaBSD; banned NC, ND, unknown, all rights reserved; per piece provenance (composer and death year, first publication year, editors, transcriber, source URL and checksum, source licence, attribution, whether modified, ingest date, takedown status); register a DMCA agent and publish a takedown address; a generated Credits and licences page. CC BY-SA does not infect the app, only adaptations of the music data (corrected notes, added fingerings), which we would release under the same licence.

**PDF reading: dropped** (decision 2026-10-28). Optical music recognition was researched and is not good enough to teach from without human review, so the library stays MusicXML and MIDI only.

**Ingestion and hosting plan** (Opus agent that ran all 38 library pieces through our importers; harness in the session scratchpad):
- **Format**: keep the source as shipped (MIDI, or piano-only .mxl) and derive the Song on the device; a packed Song format saves under 1 KB a piece over brotli (measured: 6.3 KB source, 1.3 KB brotli, 103 KB as Song JSON), so it is not worth an encoder. 5,000 pieces are about 31 MB raw or 6.5 MB brotli. Bundle a base set of 300 to 500 beginner and elementary pieces (2 to 3 MB) so the iPad has it offline; serve the rest from `quartz:/opt/piano/corpus/` (content-addressed `p/<sha>.mid|.mxl`, immutable cache headers, `catalog.json` revalidated, CORS open because the iPad origin is `capacitor://localhost`), cache fetched pieces in IndexedDB.
- **Catalogue**: `catalog.json` with composers and licences as lookup tables, about 47 bytes a row brotli'd (5,000 rows, 0.24 MB); client-side prefix search over title, composer, opus and tags with diacritics stripped.
- **Pipeline** (`scripts/library/`): fetch (resumable, polite, sha-keyed cache) then licence check then normalise to piano parts then analyse with our own importers then dedupe (composer + catalogue number, melody-fingerprint confirmation) then emit (byte-identical reruns, `LICENSES.md`). Quality gates: parse, note and bar counts, pitch range, bar lengths, declared key vs estimated key (off by 3 or more goes to review), hand-split sanity, chord and span limits, flags for D.C./D.S., repeats, odd tuplets. Automatic grade from onset rate, notes per bar, chord size, hand span, range, accidentals and rhythm (explainable terms); thresholds to be fitted on about 150 hand-labelled pieces; hand override wins.
- **Importer fixes first**: (1) minor-key MIDI signature, FIXED 2026-10-28 (the MIDI reader already returns the major key of the signature, so the extra minus three gave C minor six flats and put wrong accidentals on every minor-key MIDI piece; regression test added); (2) MIDI key changes ignored when any key event exists; (3) MusicXML written spelling (`alter`) discarded and only the first key read; (4) D.C., D.S., Fine, Coda not expanded; (5) three or more MIDI tracks fall back to a split at middle C, pin the hands at ingest; (6) irregular tuplets; (7) cue and grace notes; (8) missing tempo defaults to 100 bpm.
- **App side**: the Songs page parses every library file for thumbnails and lengths, which will not scale; take lengths from the catalogue and draw thumbnails only for cached pieces; page and search the list; per-piece credits.
- **Milestones**: M1 importer fixes 1 to 5 (2 to 3 days); M2 pipeline (3 to 4 days; first count Mutopia's real piano pieces, the 2,124 includes every instrument); M3 catalogue and hosting (2 to 3 days; move the whole-corpus parse check out of `npm test`); M4 first 500 pieces at grades 1 to 3 from Mutopia then a few OpenScore, no gate failures shipped and 2% or fewer sampled defects; M5 scale-up, PDMX only as a gated human-review queue. A realistic clean total is 1,000 to 2,500 piano pieces.
- **QA**: a perfect-player playthrough through the judge for every piece, layout checks, 5% stratified sampling by ear and eye, a Report a problem button that opens a prefilled email or issue, and an exclude list.
- **OpenScore Lieder (decision 2026-10-28: try it; tried, kept)**: voice plus piano; as piano-only it has no melody. Folding the voice line into the right hand (the importer now writes a doubled note once) passed the playability gate on 4 of the 5 songs we ship (right-hand reach at most 14 semitones for 95% of chords) and failed on Das Wandern, whose baritone line sits two octaves under the piano's right hand (reach 24), which stays piano-only. The gate is a test in `src/library.test.ts`; the same gate belongs in the pipeline.

## Known gaps

- Found by the polish pass (see `docs/changes/polish.md`): the Five-finger warm-up draws whole rests in the treble over bars 1 and 2 in the fitted and staff views; Tab can leave the open drawer and Play is the ninth stop on the start card; Progress mastery cells are 30 px wide on touch; the results still offer 'Loop bars slowly' after a clean run. Arcade leftovers kept on purpose: 'In a row' and 'Streak' counters (they are accuracy runs, not points).

- Sight Run: not yet tried on the iPad or with a real keyboard (headless Chrome with synthetic key presses only); the pace tightening is a scoring threshold, not an on-screen timer or a moving tempo; "widen when a set goes well" and "stay a note or two past comfort" are design bets, not research findings; intervals and triads are white-key only and ignore the key; the results strip is a bar chart per note, not a keyboard heatmap; Read's stage table is cramped at about 820px wide.

Play and staff:
- Staff clips ledger lines below about C2 and above about C7 (needs 8va marks).
- No tuplets or dynamics from the file on the staff; durations round to the nearest standard value.
- Hands for single-track MIDI split at one pitch, no per-note override.
- Timing windows (45, 80, 125 ms) and scoring weights are tuned by feel.
- Bluetooth MIDI adds jitter that calibration cannot remove.
- Web MIDI needs Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a computer; the iPad app has its own path.
- Cloudflare injects a Web Analytics beacon at the edge; the app sends nothing itself.

MusicXML import:
- Repeats and first/second endings are expanded and dynamics marks set velocity; da capo and segno are ignored; grace notes and unpitched percussion are skipped; transposing instruments are not transposed; time-wise scores are rejected (export partwise). Not planned.

Evidence:
- Key velocity feedback has no controlled evidence behind it; treat the Touch features as measurement, not proven pedagogy.
- The learning-science report took some details from search summaries; each such citation is marked in its bibliography. ABRSM and RCM syllabus pages were unreachable, so keys and order came from third-party charts.
